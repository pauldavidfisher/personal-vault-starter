# On Commonplace Books

For centuries, educated people kept commonplace books — notebooks where they copied out passages from their reading, organized by theme. The practice was a form of thinking. By writing something out, you made it your own.

Francis Bacon kept one. John Locke invented a system for indexing them. Thomas Jefferson filled several. They were not mere quote collections — they were tools for building a personal philosophy from the best of what had been written before.

## The digital commonplace book

Personal Vault is a digital commonplace book. The Fetch function brings texts into your library. The Clip function is the act of copying — marking the passage, making it yours, adding your note.

The difference from a paper notebook is search. A paper commonplace book requires you to remember where something is, or to build elaborate indexes. A digital vault finds anything instantly.

## What makes a good clip

A clip worth saving is one that changes how you think about something. Not merely interesting — genuinely illuminating. The test is: would you want to find this again in five years?

If yes, save it. Add a note about why it matters to you right now. Future you will thank present you.

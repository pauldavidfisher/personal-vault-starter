# On Reading

Reading is the most portable form of education. A book costs less than a meal and lasts longer than a year. The best books repay rereading — they reveal something new each time because you are new each time.

## What to read

Read widely at first, then deeply. Cast a broad net across history, science, philosophy, and literature. Then follow whatever pulls hardest.

The best guide to what to read next is what you most want to understand. If you finish a book and find yourself thinking about a particular idea, find another book that pursues it further.

## How to read

Read with a pencil. The act of marking a passage forces a decision: this matters. That decision is the beginning of understanding.

Personal Vault is built for exactly this — the pencil is the Clip button. Select any passage that strikes you, save it with a note, and it becomes part of your permanent searchable library.

## On re-reading

We never read the same book twice, because we are never the same person twice. A book read at twenty and again at forty are two different experiences. The text is fixed; the reader changes.

This is why a personal library is worth building. The books you return to are the ones that contain something you have not yet finished working out.
